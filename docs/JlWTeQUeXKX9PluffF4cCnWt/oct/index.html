Creative hub best and worst per product, 01 Oct to 31 Oct 2026
Every number here is Meta attributed revenue over GST inclusive spend. Two references are used. An entry is ranked against its own product's blended Meta ROAS, so best means above the product average and worst means below it, and a healthy entry is never called a laggard just for being the lowest of a strong set. Whether a leader is actually good is decided by that product's own brand line, shown in each tile header. The line is the product's plan ROAS target translated onto this Meta basis: plan targets are stated on MER (Shopify revenue over total spend), this section runs on Meta last-click, so each target is scaled by the account's ratio of Meta ROAS to blended MER, currently 0.84x over 0.86x = 0.97x. A product below its line is badged below line, and an entry earns Scale only if it beats its product average by 20% AND clears that line. An entry needs Rs 10,000 of spend before it is ranked, a single ad needs Rs 5,000 before it shows its own thumbnail. Click any row for the ads.

How the creative hub is built
  1. Parsing. Ad names follow Product - Format - [Partnership] - [Creator] - Angle - Description - Type - Landing - Date. The parser anchors from both ends because the middle is variable length. Creator is whatever sits immediately before Partnership, which is why lowercase handles are read correctly.
  2. Offers are excluded, Rs 90,546 in this window. PriceSlash, FreebieOffer, AOV999, LastChance and percentage-off angles are promo levers, not creative angles.
  3. Best vs worst is split on the product's own average. An entry is a leader only if it is at or above this product's blended ROAS, a laggard only if it is below. That makes it impossible for one entry to sit in both lists. Up to two a side.
  4. Per-ad floor of Rs 5,000. An entry is ranked on its aggregate spend, but inside a row only ads that individually clear Rs 5,000 get a thumbnail and a per-ad ROAS. Ads with a few hundred rupees behind them carry a meaningless ROAS, so they are collapsed into a single line instead of polluting the view.
  5. The brand line is per product (Anuj, 5 Oct 2026), not one flat number, so a 4.0x-target product is not judged by the same bar as a 1.0x-target one. Because plan targets are MER-basis and this section is Meta last-click, each is scaled by 0.97x onto the right basis rather than compared across bases. The scale is clamped to between 0.5x and 2.0x so a collapsed MER cannot produce an absurd bar. Products with no plan target fall back to 1.3x.
  6. Creative type is a thin dimension. Almost everything is Video or Static, and only a handful of products carry enough of both to compare. Read it as a sanity check, not a strategy input.
  7. Thumbnails come from Meta's CDN and the links open the ad in Ads Manager. The image URLs are signed and expire in one to four days, so they are refreshed on every daily run.

How to read this
  1. The pace marker is the point. The dark tick on every bar sits at 45%, where the month has actually got to. A fill short of the tick is behind pace, past it is ahead. Judge against the tick, never against a full bar.
  2. Product MER, not ROAS. Numerator is all channel Shopify net sales for the product including organic and quick commerce. Denominator is only the spend we could attribute to that product. Products with organic pull read high, products carrying prospecting spend read low. This will not tie to Meta reported ROAS.
  3. Total spend is (Meta + Google) x 1.18. Targets and target ROAS are stated on GST inclusive spend, so MER and its target are directly comparable.
  4. Targets are the stated figures from the Antinorm Planning sheet, one tab per month, never a pro rata. The build refuses to run unless target revenue divided by target inclusive spend reconciles to the ROAS the sheet states, which is the guard against a repeat of the September basis error.
  5. October plan. Month target is Rs 99.17 L spend incl GST against Rs 1.25 Cr revenue at 1.26x. The month window follows the plan's own week grid, so the month page equals the sum of its week pages.
  6. Google product coverage is thin. 89% of Google spend is brand, shopping or generic Pmax with no product token and sits in Unassigned, so per product Google spend is understated throughout.
  7. Smooth Operator, Splash & Dash and Shaving Grace short forms. SO, S&D and SG were all judged too ambiguous to match on, so only the spelled out names map. Ads using only the short form fall to Unassigned.
  8. Creative count is not in this version. Weekly creative targets exist in the sheet but the Meta Creatives Update tab cannot be read end to end yet.
